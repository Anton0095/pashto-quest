🌿 Laghman focus · regional notes and pronunciation limitations

What this version teaches: everyday Afghan Pashto words in the conventional Pashto script, paired with readable pronunciation approximations influenced by speech from eastern Afghanistan. Laghman is not one perfectly uniform accent: some accounts group its varieties with central Pashto, others with eastern Pashto. Treat the Latin hints as learning aids, not recordings or proof of one exact village accent.

  • ښ — commonly closer to kh (the sound in Scottish “loch”) in eastern usage, but can sound softer in some central varieties. For example: ښوونځی → khowunzay (school).
  • ږ — often has a hard g-like sound in eastern usage; speakers nearer central varieties may use a softer sound. Example: اوسېږم → osegam (I live).
  • څ — the ts cue is used for words like څنګه. Sounds can differ between nearby communities.
  • ټ / ډ / ړ — tongue curls slightly back for these consonants. In the easy reading hints, ṭ / ḍ / ṛ identify these special sounds.

🔊 Audio: Speaker buttons use pre-generated Pashto neural MP3s when installed; otherwise they use your device’s Pashto speech-synthesis voice if available. No unverified non-Pashto voice is substituted. Standard Afghan Pashto neural voices are not necessarily Laghmani; native review is still needed.

Vocabulary alternatives: خونه (khuna) also means room, while مکتب (maktab) is also used for school in Afghan conversation. The course retains کوټه and ښوونځی in its exercises.

Quality note: this is a Laghman-focused draft, not a native-speaker-verified Laghmani dictionary. Before publishing or accepting pronunciation audio as authentically local, ask a Pashto speaker from the relevant part of Laghman to review every expression. Regional descriptions: Indiana University · Pashto dialect survey.

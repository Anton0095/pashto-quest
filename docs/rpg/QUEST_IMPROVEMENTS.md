## Assessment planner

`assessment-content.js` plans answer formats and translation directions before selecting content. A 15-item assessment allocates 5 selection / 5 construction / 5 typing when enabled; disabling typing moves its five slots to selection. A 25-item assessment allocates 9/8/8 or 17/8/0. The opening expression has a selection slot. A seeded, weighted schedule checks that remaining quotas can still fit without three consecutive identical formats; the rest of the question order varies between attempts.

Construction uses English meanings and Pashto tiles. Its slots count toward the global approximately half English→Pashto target; remaining directions are distributed among selection/typing slots. No English construction tiles are generated. Content selection separately draws from the existing four-location cumulative lexical pool, current-day/focus sentence candidates and generated dialogue propositions. Dialogue propositions can be selected or typed rather than always constructed. Generated cards retain compiler proofs, licensed forms, exact morphology, deduplicated sentence texts and the existing evaluator. Construction retains exact/distractor variants. Choices exclude accepted/equivalent answers through the shared evaluator and lexical-sense authority.

If distinct licensed sentence material is insufficient, eligible vocabulary/approved phrases fill the assessment. `plan.assessmentDeficit` records intended/actual category counts, category deviations with index/reason, sentence supply and practical pool size. A fallback never manufactures grammar or changes assessment length/pass mark. Very small pools can repeat lexical targets after exhausting distinct identities. Four eligible assigned locations remain represented by recall targets, with cumulative revision and current-day sentence preferences preserved.

Library → Player Profile exposes **Enable typing challenges**, default off. It persists independently of profile name/gender. New ordinary lesson recall includes occasional independently selected typed items only when enabled; tutorial interactions remain explicit. New challenge questions freeze `answerFormat`, direction, choices/tiles and compiler proofs. Typed drafts and submitted locks resume unchanged. Changing the preference affects future tasks, including retries, while active snapshots retain their exact original questions—even when an earlier saved snapshot contains mandatory uncued recall. No new score, XP, SRS or answer-evaluation authority was introduced. Daily 15/pass-12 and weekly 25/pass-20 thresholds and first-submission/reward locks remain unchanged.

English typing uses the existing case/whitespace normalization and documented alternative senses; no automatic synonym expansion. Pashto typing uses the existing NFC normalization and explicit orthographic variants, preserving agreement/person/number/gender distinctions.

## Bilal's language review

`bilal.ts` selects optional replies from the existing sentence engine's actual unlocked forms and grammar, filtered for water/fish/river/lake content and appropriate referents, excluding unrelated shop, school and household clauses. It deduplicates texts/meanings and samples up to four options, with approved conventional greetings available when generation is sparse. An entirely new player can have only two valid greetings; no extra grammar or vocabulary is unlocked merely to fill four buttons. With compatible study/activation material, three or four replies are available. Generated replies carry their original proofs and increase in complexity only when eligible material exists. Conversations and audio do not write educational exposure, practice, SRS, mastery or XP.

From Day 4, the explicitly documented narrative fallback **کب؟ / kab?** asks about the fish Bilal has just mentioned. Its lexical source is the exact owner-approved Day 1 base `lemma:n-fish`, کب / kab; only question punctuation is added. This is a context-bound elliptical question, not an invented finite verb, morphological form, new instructional phrase or claim that the player studied the lemma. It is mechanically checked against that source and never graded. It provides a mystery-related option even when no compatible generated finite question exists. A full question/help response requires additional licensed forms and human review rather than fabricated grammar. Native review of the conversational use remains pending.

Bilal's fixed narrative lines are separate `bilal-day-1` through `bilal-day-7` events in the existing registry. Days 1–2 have short greetings/introduction; Day 3 discusses ordinary fishing; Day 4 first mentions an unusual fish; Days 5–7 give unresolved hints. Repeated interactions show the current day's short line and newly sampled replies instead of replaying a long introduction. A Day 8+ phase resolver stays on the last unresolved hook; this registers no Week 2 chapter or reveal.

The fixed lines carry Pashto, synchronized transliteration, review-only English, explicit `editorial_narrative_draft` provenance and `userVerified: false`. They are newly authored narrative drafts for the requested story, outside the approved lexical/phrase inventory. They are not native-certified, assessment material or grammar unlocks. A qualified Afghan Pashto reviewer should check spelling, naturalness, inflection and transliteration before claiming language sign-off. No reviewer or recording has been invented.

The existing dialogue renderer shows only Pashto and transliteration for these events and response buttons; review-only English is never rendered. Hear Pashto and reply playback use the shared audio service without educational tracking. Existing recording/device-voice preference and candid fallback warnings remain; no native MP3 recordings were added. Other characters retain English narrative presentation. Bilal keeps his existing lakeshore location, optional event lifecycle and nonblocking role alongside Deena's Lake lessons.

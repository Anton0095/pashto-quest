## Day 8 — This One or That One?

**Core focus:** Demonstratives: near and far. **Normal-route opening:** Saleha at Lake. **Challenge:** 15 questions, pass 12/15. Skip challenge available immediately.

**Narrative opening:** Saleha points to a book beside the player, a bird farther along the water, and two baskets at different distances. She explains that Pashto دا and هغه can point to things; هغه also occurs as he/she, so the surrounding noun and conversational context matter. Use دغه as an optional familiar local near form only after review.

### Vocabulary to introduce
- دا — this/these (proximal)
- هغه — that/those (distal)
- دغه — this/these (optional alternative; review register)
- نږدې — near (optional)
- لرې — far (optional)

**Selected review vocabulary:** book, apple, door, water, market, river, new, cold, fresh, big.

### Explicit grammar instruction
1. A demonstrative identifies which noun is meant, not who performs an action.
2. Pashto دا can modify a singular or plural noun; unlike English, it does not automatically change from this to these.
3. The demonstrative هغه and the he/she pronoun share a spelling; distinguish meaning by structure and stress (pronunciation audit).
4. Keep gender/number agreement on adjectives and equatives.

### Pashto model sentences (local review required)

| Pashto candidate | English target |
|---|---|
| دا کتاب نوی دی. | This book is new. |
| هغه کتاب زوړ دی. | That book is old. |
| دا دروازه خلاصه ده. | This door is open. |
| دا اوبه سړې دي. | This water is cold. |
| دغه کتاب نوی دی. | This book is new. (near alternative; native review) |

## Day 9 — One, Two, Many

**Core focus:** Plural nouns, agreement and small quantities. **Normal-route opening:** Edris at Market. **Challenge:** 15 questions, pass 12/15. Skip challenge available immediately.

**Narrative opening:** Edris receives a delivery and asks the player to sort individual items and groups. Singular and plural words are heard in meaningful sentences. Numbers one to five are introduced for simple counts.

### Vocabulary to introduce
- کتابونه — books (کتاب → کتابونه)
- مڼې — apples (مڼه → مڼې)
- دروازې — doors (دروازه → دروازې)
- کورونه — houses (کور → کورونه)
- یو / دوه / درې / څلور / پنځه — one to five

**Selected review vocabulary:** book, apple, house, door, student, fresh, new, big, they, this/that.

### Explicit grammar instruction
1. Pluralisation is noun-class dependent; ـونه works with many inanimate consonant-ending masculine nouns, while many ـه feminine nouns form ـې plurals.
2. Plural adjective/equative agreement must be taught with full examples; avoid guessing based on English endings.
3. دا can introduce both singular and plural noun phrases; the noun and predicate distinguish this/these.
4. Numerals and counted nouns may involve special agreement/inflection; begin with simple naming/counting tasks and speaker-reviewed sentences.

### Pashto model sentences (local review required)

| Pashto candidate | English target |
|---|---|
| دا کتاب دی. | This is a book. |
| دا کتابونه دي. | These are books. |
| دا مڼه ده. | This is an apple. |
| دا مڼې دي. | These are apples. |
| دا کورونه لوی دي. | These houses are big. |

## Day 10 — Where Is Mina’s Notebook?

**Core focus:** Here/there/where and simple spatial expressions. **Normal-route opening:** Mina at Lake. **Challenge:** 15 questions, pass 12/15. Skip challenge available immediately.

**Narrative opening:** Mina misplaces a notebook she was using beside the lake. Without adding a new playable map location, the player follows descriptions and speaks to villagers to work out where she left it.

### Vocabulary to introduce
- دلته — here
- هلته — there
- چېرته — where
- په ... کې — in/inside
- په ... باندې — on
- تر څنګ — beside (practice as a full phrase)

**Selected review vocabulary:** notebook, book, bag, school, market, chair, water, window, go, see.

### Explicit grammar instruction
1. Location questions with چېرته often keep ordinary Pashto verb placement.
2. Pashto commonly uses paired adpositions (e.g. په ... کې) rather than treating every English preposition as one Pashto word.
3. Keep the difference between a location statement and a movement-to-location sentence; school ته ځي is movement.
4. Do not introduce the full case-inflection system yet; teach fixed common phrases and mark inflection-sensitive forms for review.

### Pashto model sentences (local review required)

| Pashto candidate | English target |
|---|---|
| کتاب دلته دی. | The book is here. |
| کتاب هلته دی. | The book is there. |
| کتاب چېرته دی؟ | Where is the book? |
| مینا دلته ده. | Mina is here. |
| هغه په کور کې دی. | He is in the house. |

### Final daily challenge

**Assessment objectives:**

## Day 11 — Whose Things Are These?

**Core focus:** Possession and ownership. **Normal-route opening:** Muhammad Jan at Home. **Challenge:** 15 questions, pass 12/15. Skip challenge available immediately.

**Narrative opening:** Muhammad Jan finds household belongings mixed with items belonging to Mina and Saleha. He asks the player to identify the owner of each, building on this/that and plurals.

### Vocabulary to introduce
- زما — my/mine
- ستا — your (informal singular)
- ستاسو / ستاسې — your (polite/plural; check local preference)
- زموږ / زمونږ — our (regional variation)
- د مینا — Mina’s
- د هغه / د هغې — his / her (case and referent sensitive)

**Selected review vocabulary:** book, notebook, room, house, pen, bag, chair, this/that, we, you all.

### Explicit grammar instruction
1. Possession often uses a possessor before the noun: زما کتاب.
2. Named possessors can use د + possessor, with gender/case-sensitive forms when applicable.
3. Pashto possessives are not identical to English apostrophe-s; teach whole natural phrases.
4. Distinguish pronoun person from owner rather than allowing ambiguous his/her questions.

### Pashto model sentences (local review required)

| Pashto candidate | English target |

| دا زما کتاب دی. | This is my book. |

| دا زموږ کور دی. | This is our home. |
| دا د مینا کتاب دی. | This is Mina’s book. |
| دا د ادریس دوکان دی. | This is Edris’s shop. |

## Day 12 — Ask the Right Question

**Core focus:** Who, what, which, where, how many. **Normal-route opening:** Ms Hameed at School. **Challenge:** 15 questions, pass 12/15. Skip challenge available immediately.

**Narrative opening:** Ms Hameed runs a classroom investigation. The player learns to ask purposeful questions rather than merely answering prompts: identify people, objects, choices, places and quantities.

### Vocabulary to introduce
- څوک — who
- څه — what
- کوم — which (m.; inflection-sensitive)
- چېرته — where (revisit)
- څو — how many
- څنګه — how (recognition only, previously greeting)

**Selected review vocabulary:** student, teacher, book, notebook, market, apple, my, your, numbers 1–5.

### Explicit grammar instruction
1. Pashto question words generally appear in the position corresponding to the information requested rather than forcing English word order.
2. Yes/no questions can use rising intonation; wh-questions use interrogative words.
3. Some question words and adjectives change form with gender/case; only teach verified examples initially.
4. A question must be pragmatically natural with a sensible answer; do not test arbitrary grammar strings.

### Pashto model sentences (local review required)

| Pashto candidate | English target |
|---|---|
| دا څوک دی؟ | Who is this? (masculine referent) |
| دا څه دي؟ | What are these? |
| کتاب چېرته دی؟ | Where is the book? |
| ته څه لولې؟ | What are you reading? |
| ته کوم کتاب لولې؟ | Which book are you reading? |

## Day 13 — How We Do Things

**Core focus:** Adverbs: time, manner, frequency and group action. **Normal-route opening:** Saleha and Mina at Lake. **Challenge:** 15 questions, pass 12/15. Skip challenge available immediately.

**Narrative opening:** Saleha and Mina compare everyday routines. One walks slowly, another runs quickly, and everyone describes what they normally do at different places.

### Vocabulary to introduce
- نن — today
- سبا — tomorrow (time word only; do not teach future tense yet)
- ورو ورو — slowly
- ژر — quickly / soon (context sensitive)
- تل — always
- کله کله — sometimes
- هره ورځ — every day
- یو ځای — together (revisit)

**Selected review vocabulary:** run, walk, read, drink, school, market, we, they, I, you.

### Explicit grammar instruction
1. Adverbs add information about how, when, or how often an action occurs.
2. Do not teach “tomorrow” as a future tense form: restrict practice to timetable phrases or leave it receptive.
3. Adverbs and adverbial phrases may vary in position; accept contextually natural variants supported by the language reviewer.
4. Reuse known present-tense verbs so the new challenge is the adverb, not a new conjugation.

| موږ هره ورځ منډې وهو. | We run every day. |
| زه کله کله چای څښم. | I sometimes drink tea. |

## Implementation notes

1. Keep the original game’s location and character labels, pronunciation buttons and teaching/quiz loop. Use the English targets here to populate each day’s four contextual lessons.
2. Add new Pashto words and full sentences to the existing word bank with unique IDs and correct day/location keys. Mark the 35 model translations as *unverified* until native review; commission/review the remaining translations.
3. Do not randomly concatenate subjects, nouns and verbs into graded prompts. Curate every challenge sentence; accept valid grammatical variants when appropriate.
4. Cumulative review must include Week 1 vocabulary without overloading Day 8. Keep day-specific learning objectives distinguishable.
5. No fifth map location, inventory engine, item dragging, object placement or future tense is part of Week 2. Story events are dialogue and lesson scenes only.
6. Never infer Week 2 levels/XP from the Week 1 milestone contract. Preserve New Resident status and its graduation gate; specify Week 2 milestones in a later task.
7. Explicitly tell the player who is speaking and who they are addressing, with visible contextual cues for “this”, “that”, “you”, and “your”.

## Teacher/language QA requirements

- Pashto demonstratives distinguish near/middle/far in some analyses and share orthography with person pronouns. Do not grade orthographic homographs without context.
- Plurals are noun-class sensitive, and agreement extends to verbs/adjectives. Avoid universal “add ـونه” rules.
- Possessive case, noun obliques, adjective gender agreement, natural adposition phrases and local pronunciation require review.
- Check natural lexical selection for Laghman Afghan Pashto versus Pakistani forms. Retain standard spelling by default.
